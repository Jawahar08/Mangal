# PHASE 4 — SECOND CHAPTER PROMPT

Read the Master Prompt, feature index, repository audit, and relevant onboarding/profile/discovery/privacy implementation. Build a dignified, optional journey for people seeking a new relationship after divorce, widowhood, separation where appropriate, or as a single parent.

## Experience

Use the “New Beginning” / “Second Chapter” concept without stigmatizing users. Support an optional rebuild profile about what the person learned, wants now, what changed, what they do not want again, and current priorities. Do not force sensitive disclosures.

Provide dedicated fields and privacy controls for children, age-appropriate child details, custody/co-parenting context, family expectations, and future expectations. Protect children’s information and previous relationship details. Offer an optional private emotional-readiness reflection, with clear nonclinical framing.

## Matching and choice

Second-marriage discovery may consider previous marriage, children, geography, family and financial expectations, emotional readiness, lifestyle, and a potential partner’s acceptance of a previous marriage. Use only consented, valid data and explain the relevance of recommendations.

Allow a person to choose an appropriate Second Chapter pool or “Show me everyone” among eligible profiles; do not permanently segregate or invisibly restrict them. Honor existing preferences and safety/privacy settings.

## Support ecosystem

Organize optional resources around:

- Reset — mental wellbeing resources.
- Understand — relationship education.
- Rebuild — legal/document assistance.
- Restart — financial planning.
- Family — children and co-parenting resources.
- Reconnect — future relationship preparation.
- Heal — spiritual and wellbeing experiences.

Only list real resources or clearly label future/planned entries. Do not imply legal, financial, or mental-health advice from an unqualified provider. Connect to qualified resources only where actually available.

## Acceptance criteria

The flow feels respectful and user-controlled; users can skip, edit, and control visibility; children and custody information remain private by default; profile and matching work with or without disclosure; users can opt out of the dedicated discovery path; recommendations do not expose sensitive reasons to others. Confirm responsive/accessibility behavior and verify existing discovery eligibility rules before changes.
